Dear Mr. Montilla,

Thank you for the opportunity to put this together. Attached are two documents covering the website for Ophir Mineral Ventures.

**The proposal** is the main document. It covers what the site would include, how it would work, what it costs to run, and how long it would take. A few things worth knowing before you open it:

- **Your email is not affected.** I know that was a concern. Your domain can point email to Google and the website somewhere else at the same time — they are independent, and your existing setup stays exactly as it is. Part III explains this, with a diagram.
- **You would manage the site yourself.** Text, photos, news updates, certificate files, even your logo — all changeable from a simple editing screen without needing me. That was a firm requirement from the start.
- **Running cost is about US$75 a year** (roughly ₱4,600) — and part of that you may already be paying, since the domain appears to be registered already.
- **I looked into whether your Google subscription could host the site** to avoid a separate cost. It can't — Google doesn't support running a custom-built site inside a Workspace plan. Part III sets out why.

**The design draft** shows the intended look and feel. One important point: **everything in it is a placeholder** — the logo, the icons, the wording, the figures, the photographs and the certificate details. None of it is real information about your company, and the mark in the corner is a stand-in, not a proposed logo. It is there purely so you can judge the structure and style before we commit to anything.

Which brings me to what I'd need from you. The full list is in Part XIV, but the two that matter most are:

1. **Your logo**, ideally as the original artwork file. If nothing suitable exists in digital form, tell me and I'll design a wordmark — better to decide that early.
2. **Which registrations and permits you're comfortable publishing**, with current copies. This is the section that does the most work for buyers and banks checking you out, so it's worth getting right.

Nothing about your company will be invented to fill a gap. Until you provide the real details, those areas stay visibly blank rather than being filled with plausible-looking guesses — on a site whose purpose is being verifiable, a wrong detail is worse than an obvious gap.

There are three decisions for you at the end of the proposal: which hosting option, what support arrangement suits you, and a software version preference. Happy to talk any of them through — the hosting and support choices in particular are easier on a call than from a document.

Take your time with it, and let me know if anything is unclear or if you'd like me to adjust the approach.
