What John means by "Google host"

Untangling three different Google products that get mentally lumped together — plus what's actually true in 2026.

John almost certainly means Google Workspace — the paid product that gives him professional email like john@ophirmineralventures.com running on Gmail's infrastructure, plus Drive, Calendar and Docs. It is not a website product, even though Google bundles a very basic one inside it. Here's the full picture:

ProductWhat it actually isRelevant here?
Google WorkspacePaid subscription ($7–$22/user/month, annual) for business email on your own domain via Gmail, plus Drive/Calendar/Meet/Docs. This is what John pays for.Keep as-is
Google SitesA free, very basic drag-and-drop website builder bundled inside Workspace. Produces the generic-looking "template" sites John is reacting against. Not a CMS in any real sense — no plugins, no real SEO control, no custom design.Do not use
Google DomainsGoogle's old domain registrar. Shut down and fully migrated to Squarespace Domains in 2024 — if John registered his domain through Google before that, it now lives in a Squarespace account, not a Google one.Check where the domain actually lives now
Google Business ProfileFree listing that makes the company show up on Google Maps/local search. Separate from all of the above, and something we want regardless.Set up in launch phase

How the email part technically works

A domain's DNS zone is just a set of routing rules. Two record types matter here, and they can point to two completely different services without conflict:

  • MX records — tell the internet "mail for this domain goes to Google's mail servers." This is what makes Workspace email work, and it is completely independent of what the website runs on.
  • A / CNAME records — tell browsers "the website for this domain lives at this server." This is what we control when we build the new site.

Practical upshot: we can build a fully custom website and deploy it without touching John's email at all. The MX records stay exactly as they are; only the website-facing records change. Diagram in §04 makes this concrete.

Action for the discovery call Ask John: (1) which domain registrar he logs into today — Google, Squarespace, or something else, (2) whether he wants to keep paying for Workspace email (recommended: yes, don't touch it), and (3) whether anyone ever built a Google Site for the business that's quietly live somewhere.

Goals, audiences & requirements

Product framing before any design or stack decision.

Primary goals, in priority order

  1. Compliance & credibility — a legitimate, verifiable public presence for regulators, banks, and counterparties running due diligence.
  2. Marketing & deal-closing — professional collateral John can point buyers to instead of a WhatsApp number and a trade-directory listing.
  3. Owner independence — John edits text, swaps photos, and adds a news update himself; a developer is only needed for structural changes.

Audiences

Overseas buyers & their compliance teams

Chinese trading houses and smelters vetting a new or existing supplier before wire transfer. Want: proof of legitimacy, product specs, contact clarity.

Philippine regulators & banks

SEC, DENR-MGB, AMLA-covered banks handling large USD/RMB transfers. Want: registered name, permits, transparent ownership.

Local stakeholders & partners

LGUs, community, logistics/shipping partners, potential JV partners. Want: legitimacy, operations overview, contact.

John (the owner)

Non-developer, needs to self-serve content changes. Want: a CMS he can actually drive without calling anyone.

Non-negotiable requirements

  • Owner can edit text/images/PDFs without writing code or calling a developer
  • Loads fast and reads credibly on mobile — most Chinese buyer-side contacts will open a WhatsApp-shared link on a phone
  • HTTPS, hardened admin access, offsite backups from day one
  • Doesn't touch or risk the existing Workspace email
  • Indexable by Google/Bing (and therefore Yahoo — see §07)
  • Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) compliant contact form and privacy policy

Technical architecture

Optimized for one constraint above all others: John must be able to run this without a developer on speed-dial.

Revision — v1.1 v1 of this plan recommended WordPress, weighted mainly toward "anyone could pick this up later." That's the wrong primary criterion — the brief was a stack you already know and can run without leaning on AI or learning a new language. Revised below: Umbraco, a self-hosted, open-source CMS built on ASP.NET Core/C#. Same non-technical editing experience for John, zero new language for you.

Recommendation: self-hosted Umbraco on ASP.NET Core, deployed the way you'd deploy any of your own .NET projects — Git repo, GitHub Actions, push to a host. John gets a content tree and a block-based page editor comparable to WordPress's; you get a codebase in a language you can read, extend, and secure yourself, with no PHP and no need to loop in AI to make sense of someone else's plugin. It's also genuinely custom — no theme-marketplace look to fight against.

Why Umbraco, not a hand-rolled ASP.NET Core app

You could build the whole thing yourself in plain ASP.NET Core MVC/Razor Pages with a hand-rolled admin panel — for a single-page brochure site that's a legitimate shortcut. Umbraco earns its place the moment John needs to add pages, reorder sections, or publish a news post without you: it gives him that editing surface for free, while every template, content type, and block is still C#/Razor you write and fully understand — not a black box.

06

Security & compliance posture

Proportionate to what this site actually is: a public marketing/compliance surface, not a system of record. No customer accounts, no payments, no sensitive data at rest beyond contact-form submissions.

Transport & headers

Forced HTTPS + HSTS; Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy headers set directly in ASP.NET Core middleware — code you write and own, not a plugin default.

Admin hardening

2FA on the Umbraco backoffice, unique strong passwords, login-attempt limiting, no default admin username.

Patching & surface area

Security patches applied via NuGet package updates on your own schedule, not silently auto-applied; minimal dependency count; a staging slot for testing updates before they hit production.

Backups

3-2-1 pattern: host-level daily backup + a scheduled export of the database/media to offsite storage (Azure Blob or Google Drive), restore-tested periodically.

Data Privacy Act (RA 10173)

Published privacy policy, explicit consent checkbox on the contact form, minimal PII retention, no third-party trackers beyond GA4 without disclosure.

Role separation

John gets an "Editor" role — full content control, no ability to break plugins/theme/settings. An "Administrator" role stays with whoever maintains the site.

07

Getting found: Google, Bing & Yahoo

The useful shortcut: Yahoo Search has run on Bing's index since 2015. Covering Google and Bing covers effectively the whole market, including Yahoo and DuckDuckGo.

  1. Technical baseline — HTTPS, one canonical domain (www vs non-www decided once), XML sitemap, clean robots.txt, mobile-first responsive layout, fast load times (Core Web Vitals is a ranking factor).
  2. Submit to both consoles — Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, each pointed at the sitemap. Free, takes under an hour, and Bing Webmaster Tools covers Yahoo automatically.
  3. Google Business Profile — free local listing; puts the company on Google Maps and the local pack for searches like "nickel ore exporter Philippines."
  4. On-page SEO — unique title/meta description per page, real header hierarchy, descriptive image alt text, Organization + LocalBusiness JSON-LD schema markup.
  5. Off-page signals — get the existing trade-directory listings (Trademo, 52wmb) and any Philippine Chamber of Mines / export-association directories linking back to the new official domain.
  6. Freshness — the News/Updates page (§04) exists specifically to give search engines a reason to recrawl regularly.

Paid Google/Bing Ads are optional and out of v1 scope — worth a separate, small budget later if John wants inbound lead volume rather than relying on referral/direct.

10

Maintainability & governance

  • Editor guide — a short, plain-language one-pager for John: how to edit a page, swap a photo, publish a news post, and who to call if something looks broken. This is the single highest-leverage deliverable for "less developer intervention."
  • Roles — John: Editor role (content only). Developer/maintainer: Administrator (structure, plugins, security).
  • Staging site — every plugin/theme update tested on staging before production, so a bad update never takes the live site down mid-negotiation with a buyer.
  • Update cadence — monthly core/plugin updates, quarterly full review (broken links, outdated certs, stale stats), yearly domain/hosting renewal check.
  • Why this stack, restated — Umbraco was chosen so you can maintain, extend, and secure this site with skills you already have: no PHP, no plugin roulette, no need to loop in AI to understand someone else's code. As a secondary benefit, Umbraco has the largest .NET CMS talent pool of any option evaluated, so handing off maintenance later is still realistic — it just isn't the reason for the pick.
